The starting point

Already visible in search, but not where the clicks happen

Jenson USA sells bikes and cycling gear online. Its revenue depends heavily on category pages, the pages that answer high-intent searches like "mountain bikes". These are the searches shoppers use when they are close to buying.

The business already had search visibility and a broad product catalog. The opportunity was not building presence from zero, but converting near-miss rankings into the top positions where clicks actually happen.

Near-misses, not a blank page.

The work was about turning rankings that already existed into rankings that earn clicks.

The problem

The site was ranking, but not high enough for rankings to matter

  • Valuable keywords stuck at positions 8–10

    Category-level searches with real commercial value sat at the very bottom of page one, where click-through rates are minimal and traffic impact is negligible.

  • Thin category pages

    Category pages lacked helpful SEO content, giving search engines little reason to rank them above competitors for competitive terms.

  • Duplicate category URLs

    Uppercase and lowercase variations of the same category URLs created duplicates that split ranking signals across multiple versions of one page.

  • Limited review visibility in search

    Product review data wasn't being communicated to search engines through structured markup.

What duplicate URLs do to ranking signals

Before: uppercase and lowercase versions of the same category URL each held part of the page's ranking signals, so neither could compete as strongly as one page.

Illustrative diagram with example URLs, not measured data.

The site was doing enough to appear, but not enough to be chosen, and the highest-value category searches were delivering almost no traffic.

Execution approach

Start where the site was closest to winning

The engagement began by identifying where the site was closest to winning: keywords already ranking on page one, but at positions 8–10. These were faster, lower-risk wins than chasing entirely new keywords.

Rather than adding volume, the work focused on making existing category pages stronger and removing the structural issues holding them back.

  1. 1

    Identify high-value commercial keywords sitting just below the positions that drive traffic.

  2. 2

    Clean the foundation

    Fix duplicate category URLs first, so ranking signals are consolidated rather than split.

  3. 3

    Strengthen the pages

    Add category content and internal links to build authority on the pages that matter most.

  4. 4

    Add review schema so listings communicate trust and relevance.

Targeting keywords already close to the top made the effort efficient: small structural and content improvements were enough to move rankings meaningfully.
