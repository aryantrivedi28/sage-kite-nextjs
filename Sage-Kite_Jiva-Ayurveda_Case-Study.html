The starting point

A known brand with real demand around it, and a site that wasn't built to capture it

Jiva Ayurveda works in healthcare and wellness, offering Ayurvedic treatments and consultations for a range of medical conditions. It brought in an SEO consultant to fix a growth ceiling caused by heavy reliance on branded search traffic. The growth came from three changes:

  • A rebuilt content architecture
  • City-level local pages
  • A structured internal linking system

Its audience is made up of two groups of searchers:

  • Patients searching for disease-specific and treatment-specific solutions
  • People looking for Ayurvedic doctors and clinics in their city

When the engagement began, Jiva Ayurveda was an established brand with decent traffic, but its growth had plateaued. Most of its visibility came from people who already knew the brand name and searched for it directly, rather than from new patients finding it through disease or treatment searches.

Scaling, but capped.

Real demand existed in the market. The website wasn't built to capture it.

The problem

The issue was the type of traffic the site was getting, not the amount

  • Branded search dependence

    Most organic visits came from people typing "Jiva Ayurveda" directly. The site was capturing people who already knew the brand and wasn't winning any new demand.

  • No structured disease pages

    Patients searching for specific conditions, the highest-intent and most valuable searches in healthcare, had no dedicated, well-optimized pages to land on. That demand went to competitors.

  • No city-level pages

    Despite strong local search demand for Ayurvedic treatment "near me" or by city, no location-based landing pages existed, so entire pockets of local demand were left on the table.

  • Weak internal linking

    Diseases, treatments and cities weren't connected on the site, so even the existing content wasn't passing authority or guiding users (or Google) between related pages effectively.

How the work was done

Diagnosis first, then four layers of work

The engagement started with a diagnosis of where growth was actually stuck, rather than assuming more content or more backlinks was the answer. That meant:

  • Analyzing the traffic mix, brand versus non-brand
  • Reviewing what search demand existed for diseases and cities
  • Identifying the structural gaps keeping the site from capturing it
  1. 1

    Demand mapping first

    Understanding what patients were searching for at the disease, treatment and city level before building anything.

  2. 2

    Structural fixes

    Reorganizing the sitemap and internal linking so the site's architecture could support scale, not just individual pages.

  3. 3

    Content build-out

    Creating disease and city pages designed around real search intent rather than generic descriptions.

  4. 4

    Systemized linking

    Instead of linking pages one by one, an internal linking system was built so new disease, treatment and city pages connect to relevant related pages automatically. It stays scalable as more content is added.

Because the architecture came first, the results weren't a one-time traffic bump. The site was designed to keep compounding as more pages were added.

What changed for the business

A foundation, not a campaign

Less reliance on brand awareness

Growth used to depend on how many people already knew the brand. New patients now find Jiva Ayurveda through disease and city searches, which reduces the pressure on other marketing channels to "introduce" the brand.

A content system that sustains itself

Internal linking is automated and built into the site structure, so adding a disease or city page doesn't mean rebuilding the linking strategy. The system scales on its own.

Clearer visibility into what's working

With pages mapped to specific diseases and cities, the business can see which conditions and locations drive traffic and revenue. Future content and marketing decisions rest on data rather than guesswork.

A foundation rather than a campaign

The architecture keeps supporting growth as pages are added, and the business doesn't need a fresh SEO overhaul each time it expands into a new city or treatment area.

Takeaway

This is what happens when SEO is treated as a system instead of a series of one-off content pieces.

A healthcare brand stuck on brand-only traffic reached 6× traffic growth and 4–5× revenue growth by capturing demand it had been losing to competitors. The same approach applies to any business with strong offline trust but weak non-brand search visibility, especially in:
